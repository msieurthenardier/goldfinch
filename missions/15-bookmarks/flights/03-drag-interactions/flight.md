# Flight: Drag Interactions

**Status**: in-flight
**Mission**: [Bookmarks](../../mission.md)

## Contributing to Criteria

- [ ] Bookmarks can be dragged to reorder within the bar, dragged between the bar and the overflow menu in both directions, and dragged onto the page area to load that bookmark in the current tab (in addition to click). *(behavior-test-backed)*
  - The mission's last unmet criterion, and its only unproven mechanism.   - Met in Flights 1 and 2. This flight makes **order** operator-mutable for the first time, so the persistence clause acquires a path it has never been exercised on. `bookmarkReorder` has never run in production (DD7).

Also addresses, outside the mission's criteria:

- A **pre-existing secret-read gap** on the same surface, found during this flight's design review: `captureWindow` already composites the visible sheet into its grab (`main.js:598-615`), so at admin tier it returns pixels of `vault-unlock` and of the dismiss-locked one-time recovery-key and admin-key displays. ## Pre-Flight

### Objective

Deliver the mission's remaining criterion — bookmark drag — on real native HTML5 DnD, and settle the sheet-automation disposition that has now been deferred three times and that this flight's riskiest slice depends on. The flight opens by admitting *non-residual read* operations on the sheet's non-secret menu types — and by closing a pre-existing pixel-read gap on the same surface — then lands the four carry-forward debt items in that same unit-testable leg and closes it with one operator session that measures **two** unmeasured transports. It then builds drag in three slices: reorder within the bar, drag onto the page area, and — gated on the probe verdict — drag between the bar and the overflow menu. Every order mutation funnels through `bookmarkReorder`, which this flight is the first ever to call. It closes with a guided HAT, because the gesture under test cannot be driven by any agent in this toolchain and never will be.

**Two transports are measured before anything is built on them.** Axis (b) — does a native drag session cross chrome ↔ overlay sheet — has never been measured and gates `bar-overflow-drag`. Drag-onto-page has a *second*, cheaper question that this flight's design review surfaced: Chromium's own default handling of an un-consumed URL drop may already navigate the frame, in which case part of the criterion works today with zero code and `drag-onto-page` is a gap-fill rather than a build. They run in different sessions: DD8 closes `automation-gate`, DD5b closes `bar-drag-reorder` (which is where a draggable bar source first exists).

### Design Decisions

**DD1 — Sheet automation is admitted by (menuType × op), never by menuType alone**: Choice — `resolve.js`'s outright refusal of the sheet `WebContentsView` becomes conditional on **two allowlists, both of which must pass**: the sheet's *current* menuType is on `AUTOMATABLE_MENU_TYPES` (seeded with `bookmarks-overflow` and `bookmark-edit`), **and** the operation is one of exactly three admitted reads. Everything else — every other menuType, `null`, and every other op regardless of menuType — is refused at every tier, exactly as today.

- Rationale: the guard's stated reason (`window-registry.js:209-217`) is that **one shared** `WebContentsView` hosts the chrome-owned vault secret sheets, so refusing by wcId identity was the only available rule. It is a proxy for "this view might currently be showing a secret," and main already knows the answer — `menu-overlay-manager.js:383` exposes `getCurrentMenu() → { menuType, token, jarId? } | null`. Gating on the real question is what unblocks bar↔overflow drag verification and accessibility-tree inspection.

**DD1a — The sheet is admitted for exactly three ops, and every other op is refused** *(design-review finding, HIGH across two cycles — this reverses the original decision, then corrects its replacement)*: Choice — the sheet is admitted for **exactly** `readDom`, `readAxTree`, and `captureScreenshot`, under an allowlisted menuType. **Every other op is refused, at every tier, under every menuType.** A newly added op is refused by default.

- **This is stated additively, and that is the correction.** Cycle 2 caught that the first rework phrased the rule subtractively — three admits, three named refusals — over a funnel of **23 ops**. `resolveContents` is the single gate for every wcId-first op, and `scope.js:49-70` enumerates all of them: `closeTab`, `activateTab`, `navigate`, `goBack`, `goForward`, `reload`, `click`, `typeText`, `scroll`, `pressKey`, `captureScreenshot`, `readDom`, `readAxTree`, `evaluate`, `injectScript`, `openDevTools`, `closeDevTools`, `getZoom`, `setZoom`, `printToPDF`, `findInPage`, `stopFindInPage`, `dragPointer`. Seventeen were unclassified, and several of those are worse than the hole DD1a was written to close:
  - **`navigate(sheetWcId, url)`** replaces the shared, persistent menu-overlay document with arbitrary content that then hosts `vault-unlock`. That is strictly worse than script residency. `reload` is the same door.
  - **`printToPDF`** is a full-fidelity content read by a different door than `readDom` — the DD1c pattern repeating one flight later.
  - **`findInPage`** is a content oracle: match counts leak secret text without reading it.
  - **`typeText` / `pressKey` / `click` / `dragPointer`** are writes into an allowlisted sheet.
  - `closeDevTools` was admitted while `openDevTools` was refused, which is the tell that the enumeration was ad hoc rather than derived.
  DD1d already commits this flight to allowlist-never-denylist for menuTypes. Both halves of the gate are now stated the same way.
- **The "leaves nothing resident" phrasing is retired as the rule.** It was the *reason* for the split, and it is a bad *test* — `printToPDF` and `findInPage` leave nothing resident and must still be refused. The rule is the three-op allowlist; residency is why `evaluate` could never have been on it.

- Rationale, and it is a code fact rather than a caution: the sheet is **one WebContents with one persistent document, reused for every menu**. `ensureView()` lazily constructs once and reuses (`menu-overlay-manager.js:182-216`); `hide()` is `removeChildView` only, and the view survives (`:244-249`); `teardown()` — the only path that destroys the webContents — runs on `render-process-gone` and window close, nothing else (`:161-179`). The module header states it outright: *"the hidden sheet keeps its rendered menu DOM; the next menu-overlay:init rebuilds it"* (`:61-63`).
- **Consequence**: JS injected while `menuType === 'bookmarks-overflow'` — a `document` keydown listener, a `MutationObserver`, a monkeypatched `HTMLInputElement.value` setter — is still running when that same realm later renders `vault-unlock`, `vault-recovery-show`, `vault-adminkey-show`, or `auth-basic`. **A pre-execution check cannot revoke code that is already resident**, which is why no amount of token re-checking rescues an admitted `evaluate`. This is not a TOCTOU and would not have been caught by the original decision's own revert criterion.
- **Cost, stated honestly**: this forecloses both of the things the original DD1 was purchased for. Full axe-core auditing injects the axe source per state (`scripts/a11y-audit.mjs:283-293`) and leaves globals and observers behind — so mission Known Issue 2 is **re-scoped, not closed**: accessibility-*tree* inspection via `readAxTree` becomes reachable, axe-based auditing does not. And the DD8 probe loses `evaluate` as its instrument (see DD8 — it reverts to in-source instrumentation with a `readDom` readback, which is what Flight 1 originally specified).
- The rejected alternative, recorded so it is not re-proposed casually: reload `menu-overlay.html` on every close so no realm crosses a menu boundary. That would make injection safe and would close Known Issue 2 properly — but it converts the sheet's documented lazy-load race (the pending-init queue, `menu-overlay-manager.js:198-200`) from a once-per-session event into a once-per-open event, on a path this flight has no other reason to touch. Out of scope; the right home is a flight that owns the sheet.

**DD1b — How op identity enters, where the snapshot lives, and the post-await re-check** *(design-review finding, HIGH; mechanism named in cycle 2; **snapshot location corrected at leg-1 design review**)*: Choice — op identity enters as an **opt-in `allowSheet: true` flag spread into deps at exactly the three admitted dispatch entries**, and `sheetMenuFor` is threaded into deps as a **live reader** so the gate covers all 29 resolve points at one site. The `{menuType, token}` **snapshot** is taken **inside the two async ops**, immediately after their first `resolveContents`. Both re-check after their awaits and discard on mismatch.

- **Correction (leg-1 design review, HIGH)**: this decision originally put the snapshot in `deps()`. That is unimplementable. `deps()` (`engine.js:110-151`) is constructed with **no wcId** — the target is not known yet — and `sheetMenuFor` is inherently per-record, since `isSheetContents` walks `records()` to find *which* window's sheet matches the wc (`window-registry.js:219-227`). Under two windows a deps-time snapshot would compare window A's menu against window B's sheet, making this flight's own two-window case unsatisfiable. The "29 call sites" argument is sound but applies to the **gate**, which needs only the live reader — not to the snapshot, which exactly two ops need and which costs ~6 lines where `wc` is already in hand (`observe.js:137`, `:312`).
- Rationale for threading the gate through deps: `resolveContents` returns a bare `wc` and has **29 call sites across 7 op modules** (`tabs.js`, `observe.js`, `print.js`, `input.js`, `find.js`, `nav.js`, `zoom.js`), several of which resolve *twice* around an `activate`. A live reader in deps covers every resolve and re-resolve point without touching any call site.
- **Rationale for `allowSheet` over `deps(opName)`** *(cycle-2 finding)*: `deps()` takes no arguments and returns an identical bag for every op, so `resolveContents` has no way to know which op is asking — the menuType half has an input, the op half does not. Threading `deps(opName)` would be ~28 mechanical edits and is **fail-open**: a forgotten argument yields `undefined`, and whether that admits or refuses depends on how the predicate happens to be written. The opt-in flag inverts that: the sheet refusal stays absolute by default, three dispatch entries opt in (`engine.js:195`, `:221`, `:222`), and a new op is refused because it did nothing. That makes DD1a's failure mode **unrepresentable rather than merely documented**, and it rides the same conditional-spread idiom `isSheetContents` already uses (`engine.js:144`).
- The post-await re-check is not optional for the two async admits: `captureScreenshot` waits for paint before `capturePage()`, and `readAxTree` awaits `Accessibility.enable` then `getFullAXTree` (`observe.js:326-327`). **Both also `await activate(wcId)` first** — `classifyContents` returns `'guest'` for the sheet (`resolve.js:56-60`), so both take the guest branch (`observe.js:138-144`, `:313-318`) and then re-resolve. That is a third await window, benign today (`activateTab` returns `false` with no raise when `chromeForTab` misses) but real. **The re-check goes after the final re-resolve, covering all three awaits** — not merely before `capturePage()`. The idiom is reused verbatim from `main.js:604-609`'s "DD7 post-await re-check (TOCTOU)".
- Hidden-sheet hazard, folded into the predicate rather than left to ordering *(leg-1 design review)*: `captureScreenshot` on a **hidden** sheet hits the documented never-settles `capturePage()` case (`observe.js:145-150`, guarded by `withCaptureTimeout`). Rather than rely on the `null` refusal firing before the capture is attempted, **`sheetMenuFor` returns `null` when the sheet is not `isVisible()`** — so the hazard is disposed of structurally, and the resolver gate and `captureWindow`'s existing `isVisible()` check (`main.js:598`) become literally the same predicate, which is what DD1c already claims they are.
- The pre-execution token comparison itself mirrors `register-overlay-ipc.js:543`'s open-token freshness gate on the bookmark-edit submit channel. Proven mechanism, reused.
- **The jar tier is out of scope and stays that way**: `scope.js:138-145`'s `memberDeps()` does not carry `isSheetContents` at all — jar keys are protected from the sheet by session identity alone (`resolveContentsForJar`'s `out-of-jar`), because the sheet uses the default session. Correct today and unchanged by this flight, stated so a future partition change does not silently open a door this flight believed it had closed.


**DD1c — The same predicate closes `captureWindow`'s existing sheet layer** *(design-review finding, MEDIUM — pre-existing gap)*: Choice — `main.js:598-615`'s sheet compositing is gated on the same allowlist DD1 computes.

- Rationale: "the sheet is refused at every tier" is **already false** and has been. `captureWindow` capture-pages the visible sheet and composites it into the window grab, so at admin tier it today returns pixels of `vault-unlock` and of the dismiss-locked one-time recovery-key and admin-key displays — which render their secrets as `textContent`. That is the same secret-read vector `isSheetContents` exists to close, reached by a different door.
- Shipping DD1 while leaving this open would be incoherent: one predicate would govern DOM reads of the sheet while pixels of the same surface stayed ungoverned. Gating the existing layer costs one condition on a predicate leg 1 already computes.
- Pre-existing, and it predates this mission. Recorded as a finding of this flight's design review rather than as something the flight caused.

**DD1d — Allowlist, never a denylist; `null` refuses**: A new sheet menuType added by a future flight is refused until someone explicitly admits it. Fail-closed matches this repo's validated-no-op discipline, and the alternative silently opens each new sheet as it ships. `getCurrentMenu()` returning `null` — no menu open — refuses too.

- **Correction to the original TOCTOU rationale** *(design-review finding)*: this decision was first argued from `auth-challenges.js:442`, cited as evidence that a secret sheet can open unprompted. That citation is wrong. `:442` is inside `cancelForTab` — the path that *closes* an auth sheet — and `presentNext` explicitly refuses to present while any menu is open (`:264`, `if (record.sheet?.isMenuOpen?.()) return; // never model-replace an open menu`), with the doctrine at `:59-68`. **An auth sheet cannot model-replace an open `bookmarks-overflow`.** The real swap paths are (a) a chrome-initiated vault open after the overflow menu closes, and (b) the `null` gap between menus. The decision survives; the `null` refusal is carrying more of the load than the token re-check, and that is worth knowing when reviewing leg 1.
- Trade-off, stated plainly: this is a **security-surface change**, and it is why leg 1 takes a design review despite otherwise being mechanical work. What is given up is the simplicity of "never, at any tier, no exceptions" — a rule that needed no reasoning to audit. What replaces it needs four things to hold together (the allowlist, the `null` case, the op-class split, and the post-await re-check), and a reviewer must check all four.
- Not in scope: admitting vault, auth, or cert-picker sheets. Not now, not by this mechanism, not as an "acceptable variation."
**DD1e — Admitting `readDom` rests on a premise about the sheet's DOM that must be cited and pinned** *(design-review finding, cycle 2)*: Choice — leg 1 records the co-residency premise with citations and adds an assertion that a read under an allowlisted menuType cannot return secret material.

- Rationale: this is the exact mirror of the argument that reversed the original DD1. The sheet's single document holds **every menu's card DOM** — `menu-overlay.html:18` is one `#menu-root` and each template appends its own node into it. If a `vault-recovery-show` card's `textContent` were still present when `bookmarks-overflow` renders, then `readDom` — the op DD1a *admits* — would return the one-time recovery key.
- **It doesn't, and the mitigation is real**: `onInit` calls `menuController.closeAll()` before rendering (`menu-overlay.js:2317`), and the three secret cards scrub on close — `recovery.keyValue.textContent = ''` (`:1224`), `accessKey.secretValue.textContent = ''` (`:1423`), `adminKey.keyValue.textContent = ''` (`:1484`). The conclusion survives.
- **But the premise is load-bearing, was uncited, and is pinned by no test** — nothing today stops someone removing that scrub, because until this flight nothing could read the sheet's DOM at all. Leg 1 adds the assertion: a `readDom` under `bookmarks-overflow`, taken immediately after a `vault-recovery-show` was open, contains no key material.
- One empirical check in leg 1's operator session, cheap and worth doing: `vaultInput.value` is cleared on **onOpen**, not onClose (`menu-overlay.js:618`), so a typed master password survives in the input's `value` *property* after the vault sheet closes. `readDom` is expected safe (`outerHTML` does not serialize a property-assigned value — `observe.js:160-164`) and `readAxTree` is expected safe (`display:none` subtrees are absent from the AX tree, and Chromium does not expose password values). Expected is not measured, it is a secret, and the session is happening anyway.


**DD1f — The sheet's DOM is scrubbed EAGERLY on close, not lazily on the next open** *(leg-1 design review cycle 2, HIGH — operator ruling 2026-08-05)*: Choice — `closeMenuOverlay` sends the sheet a close/reset message that runs `report.silence()` then `menuController.closeAll()` immediately. The sheet's document never retains a closed menu's content.

- **This closes a hole that would have made DD1e false in practice.** `deliverInit` is the **only** message main sends the sheet (`menu-overlay-manager.js:151`, called at `:203`/`:310`) — there is no close message. `openMenu` sets `currentMenu` and calls `show()` **synchronously** (`:298-302`), then `deliverInit` (`:310`) crosses an IPC hop; the scrub runs in the renderer *after* that (`menu-overlay.js:2317`). Between those two points `sheetMenuFor(wc)` reports an admitted menuType while the DOM still holds the previous secret menu's `textContent`. An admin client can drive the chrome to open the chevron and loop `readDom` across it.
- The renderer's own comment names the case: *"re-open of a persisted DOM after a **main-initiated close**"* (`menu-overlay.js:2312-2315`). Main-initiated closes (`tab-switch`, `tab-hide`, `tab-close`, `superseded`, `teardown`) close even a `dismissible:false` card (`menu-overlay-manager.js:334-338`), so the residue path is reachable without any user action on the sheet.
- **Why this is a design fix and not an implementation detail**: it is exactly the flight's own divert condition — *"any path by which an admitted op reads a secret from the shared sheet realm."* And it is **unobservable by the verification this flight has**: the window is orders of magnitude narrower than human timing, so both AC7's Grep-AC and the operator's live check would pass while the premise was false. A checkpoint that cannot fail is not a check.
- Trade-off: the leg grows to touch `menu-overlay-manager.js` and `menu-overlay.js`, and this flight otherwise has no reason to open the sheet's own machinery. Accepted — the alternative was documenting that an admitted read op can return vault secrets under a race, which undercuts most of what DD1 buys.
- `<body>` data attributes are untouched by `closeAll()`, so DD8's probe readback premise survives unchanged.
- Consequence for leg scoping: the four carry-forward debt items move to their own leg (**DD10 amended**), so the security diff is reviewable on its own.


**DD2 — Native HTML5 DnD, one drag-session model, `application/x-goldfinch-bookmark`**: Choice — bookmark drags use native HTML5 DnD end to end, following `tab-controller.js`'s shape: `draggable` bar items, a session object snapshotted at `dragstart`, `dragover` for the preview, `drop` for the same-surface commit, `dragend` for release-point disambiguation and cleanup.

- Rationale: it is the only transport that works. Synthetic pointer injection cannot initiate a native drag (`docs/mcp-automation.md`, M09 F11 Leg 2), and it is the transport Flight 1's spike **measured** crossing into the guest. It is also the transport already in this codebase, with its non-obvious rules learned the expensive way — most notably that `e.dataTransfer.dropEffect` must be set in `dragover` or the drop is silently rejected (`tab-controller.js:499`, "MANDATORY (spike probe3)").
- **`dropHandled` discipline carries over verbatim.** `drop` fires before `dragend`; a synchronous flag set in `drop` is what stops `dragend` from double-committing (`tab-controller.js:524`). Bookmark drag has the same two-outcome shape and takes the same guard.
- **The dataTransfer carries three types, deliberately:**

  | Type | Payload | Read by |
  |---|---|---|
  | `application/x-goldfinch-bookmark` | the bookmark's **id** | chrome only, via `getData` — reorder and bar↔overflow dispatch |
  | `text/uri-list` | the bookmark's url | any page with its own drop zone |
  | `text/plain` | the bookmark's url | same |

  The two standard types are what make DD5's "page wins" *mean* something: a page that accepts the drop gets a normal url drop and behaves as it would in any browser, which is the whole point of letting it win. The custom type is the chrome's own dispatch key.
- **The guest reads `types`, never `getData`** *(design-review correction)*: an earlier draft said the guest never reads the custom type at all. That is not implementable — the guest's `dragover` handler **must** gate on `e.dataTransfer.types.includes('application/x-goldfinch-bookmark')`, or it preventDefaults every drag on every page and breaks native file and link drops. `types` is readable during `dragover` under the HTML5 drag protected mode; `getData` is not, and the guest never calls it. The distinction is exact and load-bearing for DD6: the guest can *recognise* a bookmark drag and can never *read* what bookmark it is.
- Trade-off: a page can read the dragged bookmark's url off a real drop, from the standard types. That is intended — the operator deliberately dropped it there — and it is what a standard link drag already does everywhere else.

**DD3 — Drop-index math reuses `dropIndexFromPointer`; the new pure module holds slot assembly and drop classification, NOT an index translation** *(rewritten after design review — the original justification was false)*: Choice — `src/shared/tab-order.js`'s `dropIndexFromPointer(slotRects, pointerX, draggedIndex)` is called unchanged for the midpoint rule; a new `src/shared/bookmark-drag.js` holds the bookmark-specific pure logic.

- Rationale for the reuse: `dropIndexFromPointer` (`tab-order.js:107-118`) has **no tab semantics** — it takes `{left, width}` rects and an x. It is already unit-pinned, and its tie rule ("exactly on a midpoint does not count") is already reasoned about. Re-deriving it would produce exactly the hand-mirrored-pair-without-a-differential-test shape both prior debriefs flag as this codebase's recurring defect source.
- **Correction — there is no visible→full index translation to write, because the two index spaces are identical.** The original decision claimed `itemEls()` and `listFor(jarId)` diverge whenever `overflowing === true`. They do not: `itemEls()` (`bookmarks-bar.js:149-151`) returns **all** `.bm-item` children in full-list order, and the hide is a strict tail — `items.forEach((el, i) => el.classList.toggle('hidden', i >= visibleCount))` (`:245`). Visible index **is** full-list index for every visible item; the claimed translation is the identity function. Had this shipped, leg 2 would have unit-tested a function with no terms, and the Flight 2 fixture rule would have been applied to it — a fixture in which *every term changes the answer* is unsatisfiable when there are no terms.
- **The real requirement, which the original decision missed**: `.bm-item.hidden` is `display: none` (`styles.css:729-731`), so hidden items report **zero-width rects at left 0**. Feeding the raw `itemEls()` rects into `dropIndexFromPointer` counts every hidden item (midpoint `0 < pointerX` for any pointer past the bar's left edge), inflating the index by exactly the overflow count. What leg 2 needs is **slot assembly that filters hidden items before measuring** — a different function with a different failure mode, and one where a discriminating fixture is meaningful (a bar with items both before and after the pointer, *and* hidden items, is the case that separates a correct implementation from a plausible one).
- What `bookmark-drag.js` is therefore actually for: (a) hidden-filtered slot-rect assembly, (b) drop classification (bar run / chevron / outside the bar entirely), (c) for leg 4, the external-source insertion case where the dragged item is not in the rect array at all. It follows `tab-drag-zone.js`'s division of labour: pure, no DOM, no Electron, unit-tested offline; the renderer owns only the DOM reads that produce its inputs, and degenerate inputs fail to the non-destructive outcome.
- **The external-source case needs `dropIndexFromPointer`'s contract extended, not assumed.** For an overflow→bar drag the dragged slot is absent from the rect array; passing `draggedIndex = -1` yields the correct plain insertion index, but the documented contract says "the insertion index **among the remaining slots**" (`tab-order.js:88-92`), which presumes the dragged slot is present. Leg 4 extends the JSDoc and pins the external-source case in `tab-order.test.js`, so a future reader does not "fix" a behaviour two features now depend on.
- Trade-off: two features now depend on one predicate. Accepted — that is the intent, and a change to the midpoint rule *should* be visible to both.
- Acceptable variation, stated because the justification shrank: if (a)–(c) reduce to less than a module's worth once `bar-drag-reorder` designs them, inlining them in `bookmarks-bar.js` is fine. The purity and the unit tests are the requirement; the file is not.

**DD4 — Overflow membership stays derived; `bookmarkReorder` is the only mutation any drag performs**: Choice — dropping a bar item onto the chevron or the open overflow sheet reorders it to a position at or past the last visible slot; dropping an overflow row onto a bar slot reorders it to that slot's full-list index. No stored overflow flag, no schema change.

- Rationale: overflow membership is a *rendering* fact derived by `partitionOverflow` from measured widths. Storing it would create a second source of truth that the width partition would immediately contradict on the next window resize, and it would break the bar's own invariant that it shows as much as fits.
- **Consequence, and it is the user-visible one**: dragging an item *out of* overflow onto the bar pushes whatever was last-visible *into* overflow. That is correct — the bar's capacity did not change — but it will read as surprising the first time, and the HAT should confirm it reads as sensible rather than as a bug.
- Trade-off: the gesture is not literal. What the operator dragged into overflow does not *stay* in overflow across a window widen; it reappears on the bar. Accepted: the alternative is a stored flag that fights the layout on every resize.
- Because the store mutation is `reorder` in all cases, **every drag path shares one failure mode and one broadcast** — a jar-scoped `bookmarks-changed`, re-derived by the existing cache path. No new invalidation channel.

**DD5 — Drag onto a page: the page wins, and the `defaultPrevented` read is deferred to a macrotask** *(discriminator mechanism corrected after design review)*: Choice — the page keeps any drop it consumes; the bookmark loads only when nothing consumed it. The discriminator is `e.defaultPrevented`, read **after the whole dispatch has finished** — never synchronously inside the handler.

- Rationale for the policy: a page with a real drop zone — a mail composer, an upload target — calls `preventDefault()` as the HTML5 spec requires in order to accept a drop, and that flag is a standardised signal that the drop was consumed. This is the behaviour of every mainstream browser and it makes goldfinch a correct web citizen rather than one that silently breaks drop targets. Paired with DD2's `text/uri-list`/`text/plain`, a page that wins the drop gets a **normal url drop** and does something useful with it — the two decisions only make sense together.
- **Correction — "bubble phase means page handlers run first" is false here, and the original formulation would have destroyed the drops it exists to protect.** Bubble ordering only puts page handlers first when they sit on nodes *below* `document`. The preload runs at document-start, **before any page script**, so a preload listener on `document` or `window` is first in registration order on that node and fires *ahead of* a page handler on the same node. `document.addEventListener('drop', …)` is the single most common shape for a global dropzone. In that case the preload would read `defaultPrevented === false`, navigate, and destroy the page's drop — exactly the failure mode this decision exists to prevent.
- **The ordering-independent formulation**: capture the event in the handler and read `e.defaultPrevented` from a `setTimeout(…, 0)` — a fresh macrotask, guaranteed to run after the entire dispatch. `queueMicrotask` is **not** sufficient and must not be substituted: a microtask checkpoint occurs after each listener callback whenever the JS stack is empty, and for a browser-dispatched event the stack *is* empty between listeners, so a microtask can run before the next listener does. This is a real distinction, not pedantry, and it is the kind that is invisible until a specific page breaks.
- Belt and braces, cheap: register on `window` in the bubble phase rather than `document`, so the handler is last in the propagation path as well as deferred. The deferral is what makes it correct; the position just narrows the window. It also buys a second, free discriminator: a page that calls `stopPropagation()` on `drop` at a lower node prevents the preload handler from running at all — the right outcome, reached without any `defaultPrevented` logic.
- **`dataTransfer` reads stay synchronous** *(design-review finding)*: `e.dataTransfer` leaves protected mode when dispatch ends, so the `types.includes('application/x-goldfinch-bookmark')` gate must be captured into a local **inside** the handler and read from the closure in the deferred callback. Only `defaultPrevented` is read late. Easy to get wrong precisely because the deferral makes "read it later" feel uniform.
- Trade-off: a page can suppress bookmark-drag navigation by preventDefaulting drops it does not actually use. Accepted — the operator sees the drop do nothing and clicks the bookmark instead, and the alternative (capture phase, `stopPropagation`, chrome always wins) breaks legitimate pages to defend against a hypothetical annoying one.
- Edge case `drag-onto-page` must settle: `dragover` must be preventDefaulted for `drop` to fire at all (the exact HTML5 requirement that made Flight 1's first probe read like a failure — the highest-value lesson of that flight), and it must be gated on `types.includes(…)` per DD2, or every drag on every page gets preventDefaulted and native file and link drops break.

**DD5b — Measure Chromium's own default before building any of it, at the END of `bar-drag-reorder`** *(design-review finding; its placement corrected in cycle 2)*: Choice — a checkpoint at the end of leg 2, before any leg-3 preload code exists, measures what happens when a bookmark is dragged from the bar onto an ordinary page.

- Rationale: Chromium's default handling of an un-consumed URL drop on a page is to navigate the frame. Flight 1's spike already proved the drop *arrives* in the guest with the custom MIME intact. If the default fires and routes through the app's existing `will-navigate` / `isSafeTabUrl` guard (`guest-wiring.js:236`), then **DD5's policy is satisfied by construction** — a page that consumes the drop keeps it, a page that doesn't gets navigated by the engine — and DD6's entire no-authority signalling apparatus is unnecessary. Leg 3 shrinks to a gap-fill, possibly to nothing.
- If instead the default does not fire, or fires but bypasses the navigation gate, `drag-onto-page` builds the DD5/DD6 path as specified and now knows *why*.
- **Why `bar-drag-reorder` and not `automation-gate`, corrected**: an earlier draft put this in the gate leg and called it "zero code changes." **That was false and would have produced a false negative.** `buildItemButton` (`bookmarks-bar.js:158-190`) creates a plain `<button class="bm-item">` — there is **no `draggable = true` anywhere in the file**, and buttons are not draggable by default (contrast `tab-controller.js:117`, where the tab strip sets it explicitly). In leg 1 the operator could not start a drag at all, would report "nothing happened," and the flight would have recorded *"Chromium's default does not fire"* when in fact **no drag session existed** — DD8's own negative-probe failure mode, reproduced inside the decision adopted from DD8's discipline, gating leg 3's entire shape. `bar-drag-reorder`'s DD2 work gives the bar a real `draggable` source carrying `text/uri-list`, so at the end of that leg the probe is *genuinely* zero-instrumentation.
- The binding constraint was never the gate leg — it is **before `drag-onto-page`**, because once the preload preventDefaults `dragover`, Chromium's default is suppressed and the IPC path replaces it. The end of `bar-drag-reorder` satisfies that with room to spare.
- **Negative-probe audit, mandatory** (DD8's rule, applied to itself): before writing down "the default does not fire," confirm a drag session actually started. A `dragstart` that never fired and a default that never fired look identical from the operator's chair.

**DD6 — The guest's drop signal carries no authority**: Choice — the guest sends a bare "a bookmark drop landed in this tab" IPC with **no url and no id**. Chrome resolves what was dragged from its own live drag session. Main verifies a bookmark drag was declared at `dragstart` before forwarding, and refuses otherwise.

- Rationale: `contextIsolation` is **off** in guest tabs (`webview-preload.js` runs in the page main world), so a hostile page can fabricate a `DragEvent` and reach the preload's handler directly. If the payload carried a url, that would hand every page a primitive to navigate its own tab to a url of its choosing — and the mission's constraint that bookmark activation never becomes a web-reachable path into internal pages would rest entirely on the untrusted-navigation gate holding. A signal that carries no data cannot be aimed.
- **The bookend gate is not new.** `register-tab-ipc.js:642-678` and `window-registry.js:96-97` already implement exactly this for tab adopt-by-drop: `dragstart` declares the wcId, main verifies the declaration, a forged MIME payload dies at `'not-dragging'`, and `dragend` clears on a grace timer rather than immediately. Bookmark drag registers its own declaration alongside it.
- Worst case for a hostile page: outside a real drag session there is no session to resolve, so the signal resolves to nothing. Inside one, the operator is deliberately dragging a bookmark and the page can at most cause the navigation the operator was already performing.
- The `isTrusted` capture idiom (`webview-preload.js:231-234`) applies here too — annoyance hardening, honestly labelled as such, not a security boundary.
- **The declaration needs its own field, not the tab drag's** *(design-review finding)*: `dragWcId` is a **single slot per window record** (`window-registry.js:99`). A bookmark drag reusing it would clobber a tab drag's declaration. Leg 3 adds a distinct field; "alongside" means beside, not shared.
- **The chrome-side session needs a grace window past its own `dragend`** *(design-review finding, MEDIUM)*: the bookend gates *forwarding*, but the thing that resolves the url is the chrome renderer's in-memory drag session — and the guest `drop` → main → chrome hop crosses two processes and two IPC pipes with **no ordering guarantee against the chrome's own `dragend`**. If `dragend` nulls the session first, the navigation resolves to nothing, intermittently. This is the same cross-pipe hazard the tab code's own comment names as the reason its main-side declaration clears on a 1500 ms grace timer (`register-tab-ipc.js:647`, `:665-671`) rather than immediately. Leg 3 retains the resolved bookmark past `dragend` for a bounded window, or resolves against a snapshot taken at `dragstart`. Not optional — an intermittent no-op is the worst failure shape available here, because it will be reported as "sometimes drag doesn't work" and will not reproduce under a debugger.

**DD6b — The reorder payload is re-read from the store before commit, never derived from the cache alone** *(design-review finding, MEDIUM)*: Choice — a drag commit `await`s `bridge.bookmarksGet({ jarId })` and builds its full id list from that, rather than from `bookmarksClient.listFor(jarId)`.

- Rationale: `listFor` is a synchronous read of a cache refreshed only by the `bookmarks-changed` round trip (`bookmarks-client.js:101-112`). DD7 mandates sending **full, complete** id lists. Combine the two and a drag becomes a read-modify-write over possibly-stale state: if another window added a bookmark and this window has not yet processed the broadcast, the payload omits that id, and the store's forgiving rule appends it **at the end** (`bookmarks-store.js:268-271`) — silently relocating another window's bookmark to last position, returning `{ ok: true }`, and broadcasting the damage as if it were intended.
- The store's tolerance is a safety net against malformed input, not a licence to send stale input. It cannot distinguish "this id was omitted because the caller didn't know about it" from "this id was omitted deliberately."
- Cost: one `await` on a gesture that already consumed a second of human time. There is no perceptible latency budget being spent here.
- **Cache freshness contract for the drag path, stated in full**: source of truth is the main-side `bookmarks` table; the chrome cache's rebuild triggers are unchanged (first read of a jar, jar-scoped `bookmarks-changed`, `jars-changed` eviction); maximum staleness is one broadcast round trip; and the drag commit **does not read through the cache at all** — it reads through `bookmarksGet` and is therefore a last-writer-wins rewrite over state no older than its own round trip. Rendering still reads the cache; only the mutation's input does not.

**DD7 — `bookmarkReorder` is unproven code, and `bar-drag-reorder` treats it as unproven**: Choice — that leg verifies the full reorder path live before building UI on top of it, rather than assuming a green unit suite means a working path.

- Rationale: the Flight 2 debrief states it plainly — *"zero renderer call sites; the entire DD2 position machinery is unit-test-only. Flight 3 is its first consumer and must treat it as unproven."* The path spans `bookmarks-client.js` (no call site at all today) → `chrome-preload.js:68` → `register-bookmarks-ipc.js:98` → `bookmarks-store.js:251` → `app-db.js:605`'s `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction. Leg 2 sends **full, complete id lists**, never partial ones — the forgiving behaviour is a safety net, not an interface.

## In-Flight

### Technical Approach

The flight opens the instrument, then builds on the measured transport, then builds on the one it had to measure itself.

**Instrument first.** Leg 1 changes `resolve.js`'s sheet refusal from an identity test to a two-part test: the sheet's current menuType against an allowlist, and the op's class. `sheetMenuFor(wc)` comes out of `window-registry.js` (which already walks the records to find the matching sheet) and into the engine opts by the same conditional-spread idiom `isSheetContents` rides (`engine.js:144`), as a **live reader**; the `{menuType, token}` snapshot is taken **inside the three admitted ops** after their first resolve, because `deps()` has no wcId to snapshot against (DD1b); all three get a post-await re-check on the idiom `main.js:603-607` already models. `closeMenuOverlay` gains an eager scrub message so the sheet never retains a closed menu's DOM (DD1f). `captureWindow`'s existing sheet layer gets the same predicate. Leg 1b lands the four carry-forward debt items, all mechanical. Leg 1 closes with one operator session carrying the DD8 probe.

**Chrome-internal drag.** `bar-drag-reorder` adds `src/shared/bookmark-drag.js` — pure, no DOM, no Electron, `tab-drag-zone.js`'s shape — holding hidden-filtered slot assembly and drop classification, delegating the midpoint rule to `tab-order.js`. `bookmarks-bar.js` gains the session model and the four handlers, an insertion indicator, and the first-ever call to `bookmarkReorder`, whose payload is re-read through `bookmarksGet` rather than the cache. Order changes re-derive through the existing jar-filtered `onChanged` path; no new invalidation channel. Both `render()` and the `ResizeObserver` re-partition are suppressed while a session is live, because either one rebuilds the drag source out from under the gesture.

**Cross-surface drag into the guest.** `drag-onto-page`'s shape is decided by the DD5b baseline measured at the close of `bar-drag-reorder`. In the build case it adds `dragover`/`drop` listeners to `webview-preload.js` — the first gated on `types`, the second deferring its `defaultPrevented` read to a fresh macrotask — firing a bare signal, plus a declared-session bookend in main on its own field beside the tab drag's, and a chrome-side subscriber that navigates from a drag session held past its own `dragend`. The `dragover` handler must preventDefault without stealing the page's chance to accept — the exact rule that made Flight 1's first probe read like a failure.

**Cross-surface drag into the sheet.** `bar-overflow-drag` exists only if the probe says it can. If it can, the sheet's rows become drop targets and drag sources, dispatching through the existing snapshot; if it cannot, the leg is replaced by a mission amendment.

**Verification.** One new HAT-apparatus behavior spec, the sheet a11y audit DD1 unblocks, and a guided HAT — because the gesture is the operator's and no amount of design changes that.

### Adaptation Criteria

**Divert if**:

- The DD8 probe returns not-viable **after** the instrument audit. `bar-overflow-drag` is replaced by a mission amendment renegotiating criterion 6's bar↔overflow clause to click-only. This is pre-authorized (mission Open Question 3) but it changes a mission criterion, so it is a diversion with an operator confirmation, not an acceptable variation.
- Any path is found by which an **admitted** op leaves state in, or reads a secret from, the shared sheet realm — the DD1a class of defect, of which the resident-injection case is the one already found. **Revert DD1 entirely** and fall back to formally accepting the sheet limit; do not ship a partial gate. Leg 4 then proceeds operator-verified only, and sheet a11y stays out of reach. Note this criterion is deliberately broader than "the token re-check has a hole" — the original decision's revert criterion was written that narrowly and would not have caught the defect that was actually there.
- `bookmarkReorder`'s live behaviour contradicts its unit tests (DD7) in a way that implies the store's position model is wrong rather than the renderer's call being wrong — that is a Flight 2 defect surfacing, and it re-plans rather than gets patched in a drag leg.
- Native drag proves unusable for the bar because the bar's own `ResizeObserver` re-partition fires mid-drag and destroys the drag source. If this cannot be resolved by suppressing re-partition during an active session, escalate — the mechanism is wrong, not the tuning.
- Drag-onto-page cannot be made safe without the guest payload carrying a url. That contradicts DD6's premise and needs re-planning, not a relaxation.

**Acceptable variations**:

- The insertion indicator's visual treatment (line, gap, ghost) — provided it is instant and does not animate guest layout.
- Whether `bookmark-drag.js`'s three pieces (hidden-filtered slot assembly, drop classification, the external-source insertion case) live in their own module or inline in `bookmarks-bar.js`, provided each is pure and unit-tested. The purity and the tests are the requirement; the file is not.
- Which additional non-secret menuTypes seed DD1's allowlist, provided every one is confirmed secret-free and the default stays refuse.
- Exact wording of the `CLAUDE.md` additions.
- Whether `BAR_GAP`/`BAR_PADDING_X`/`CHEVRON_WIDTH` are exported for the pin test or the test scans the source.
- Auto-scroll behaviour when dragging past the bar's edge — desirable, not required.
